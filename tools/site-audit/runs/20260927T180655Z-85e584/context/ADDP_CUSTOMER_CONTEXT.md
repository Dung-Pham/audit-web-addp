## Audience and decision context

Primary buyers or users may include middle-aged adults, older adults, and adult children researching or purchasing products for parents. The product user and purchase decision-maker may differ. Evaluate both an older or middle-aged person who may consume the product and an adult child who researches, compares, verifies trust, and purchases for a parent.

Do not assume visitors are young, digitally fluent, visually sharp, or familiar with current ecommerce patterns. Do not stereotype older users or assume inability. Report only observed barriers and evidence-backed impacts.

## Persona overlays

### First-time visitor

Represent a first-time middle-aged or older visitor. Determine what ADDP is, what it offers, who products appear intended for, why the visitor should trust the company or product, how to reach a relevant product, and the next obvious action. Assess legibility, navigation, product differentiation, information hierarchy, trust signals, CTA clarity, animation, visual complexity, and wayfinding.

### High-intent buyer

Evaluate both an older or middle-aged self-purchaser and an adult child purchasing for a parent. Seek product identity, intended user, price, package or quantity, promotions, purchase information, CTA, cart path, visible checkout path, and support. Assess whether the site provides enough information and trust to buy for another person, and whether purchase can proceed without the older product user operating checkout. Stay read-only and stop before cart mutation, order, checkout submission, payment, account creation, or any other state change. Record the exact stop point.

### Mobile / low-tech user

Represent a middle-aged or older phone user with ordinary or limited digital confidence. At the recorded mobile viewport assess text without zoom, contrast, font size, line height, spacing, density, navigation, touch targets, labels versus ambiguous icons, scroll burden, sticky elements, overlays, movement, precision demands, hidden gestures or hover behavior, carousels, product and price visibility, CTA visibility, help routes, orientation, and recovery from a wrong path.

### Research / skeptical buyer

Represent a digitally capable adult child researching a nutrition product for a parent. Seek product identity, intended audience, ingredients, stated uses and benefits, claims, sources, declarations, certifications, public documents, company identity, credentials, reviews, feedback, photos or videos, FAQ, appropriate-use boundaries, price, promotions, support, and purchase information. Distinguish website-present information, support shown or linked by the website, and unknowns. Do not infer medical validity, clinical effectiveness, authenticity, or individual suitability.

## Shared trust and age-related focus

Check small text, low contrast, thin fonts, hierarchy, dense copy, small controls, color-only meaning, animation, auto-moving content, hover or gesture dependencies, ambiguous icons, carousel speed, tap precision, CTA wording, hard-to-find product or price information, company identity, trust evidence, support routes, product documents or certifications, ingredient transparency, health and nutrition claims, product and price consistency, review evidence, and purchase-path clarity.

For health and nutrition products, conversion includes confidence: clear company and brand identity, real product imagery, appropriate real-person imagery, public product evidence, ingredient transparency, understandable explanations, credible feedback, support routes, consistent product and price information, and claims proportionate to the support shown.
